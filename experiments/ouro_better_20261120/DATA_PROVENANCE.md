# Iteration 1 data provenance

The two source files are ARC-Challenge official Train at revision `fdb98c79578d61bdf502e2ade3253c71154dd11f` and OpenBookQA `main/train` at revision `388097ea7776314e93a529163e0fea805b8a6454`. [The frozen split manifest](SPLIT_MANIFEST.json) includes source checksum, selected question identifiers, duplicate cluster identifiers, content hashes and rejected identifiers. Preparation projects ID, stem and choices from source parquets. The training and evaluation runners read answer keys only for the selected cohort.

The project previously collected native trajectories on all 1,119 ARC-Challenge Train IDs. This iteration uses disjoint within-run ARC cohorts for fitting (512), development (128) and diagnostics (256). The 25 historical demonstration stems are excluded. A 351-ID prior targeted-exposure inventory, including historical demonstrations, defines fit-only exposure. Repeated or near-repeated question contents are grouped before selecting subjects by hash. [Prior ARC exposure audit](provenance/arc_targeted_exposure.json)

The only blind confirmation in this iteration is 384 questions from OpenBookQA official Train. Historical OpenBookQA experiments recorded zero-shot validation and test scoring. The project had downloaded the original Train file. The provenance review found no historical Train scoring or demonstration use, and excluded 35 Train IDs with exact normalized stem matches to historic validation and test and two additional near duplicates based on identical unordered choices and stem similarity. [Prior OpenBookQA intersection](provenance/openbook_prior_exclusions.json)

The full reproducibility inputs are the two revision-pinned official Train parquets, the previously released [six-protocol freeze](../ouro_hidden_computation_20261120/protocol_results/PROTOCOL_FREEZE.json) and the fixed [demonstration record](../ouro_latent_state_mechanism_v1/research_plans/latent_dynamics_continuation_20261113/E7_natural_reencoding/source_and_protocol/REENCODING_DEMONSTRATIONS.json). Exact matching uses normalized NFKC case-folded word sequences. Near-duplicate matching requires the same reordered-normalized choice set, sufficiently long stems and a SequenceMatcher similarity of at least 0.90. [Split implementation](prepare.py)
