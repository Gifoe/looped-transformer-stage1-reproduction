# Methodological and contribution boundary

Activation patching and mediator restoration are established methods, not inventions of this experiment. Vig et al., [Causal Mediation Analysis for Interpreting Neural NLP: The Case of Gender Bias](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12265), explicitly study direct and mediated effects through model components and interactions between them. Our controlled four corners are an application to a recurrent-model state transition. We do not claim a new general causal identification method.

Zhang and Nanda, [Towards Best Practices of Activation Patching in Language Models: Metrics and Methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16042), report that metric and corruption choices can substantially change interpretability results. This motivates preserving continuous candidate gaps, decisions, ties, donor controls and both intervention directions here. In our data, large residual-held-attention gap losses coexist with meaningful attention-mediated decision recoveries; reducing those outcomes to one preferred metric would obscure the finding.

The specific contribution presently supported is an audited, receiver-dependent account of one Ouro-1.4B L18 readout computation on the fixed ARC setup: transplantable KV effects, subsets of natural Query/KV dependence, and controlled R-state→actual-attention restoration with a remaining residual/suffix route. This is more specific than an accuracy comparison, but is not a complete explanation of where the second loop creates the useful state. A claim that this is the first such recurrent-model result would require a broader literature review and is not made. This targeted methodological check does not establish priority over all existing work or publication readiness.
